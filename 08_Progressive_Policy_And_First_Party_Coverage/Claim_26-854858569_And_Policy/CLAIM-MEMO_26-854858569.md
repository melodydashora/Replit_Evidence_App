## Assessment
*Analysis prepared for counsel from the documents in this repository. Not legal advice; no attorney has reviewed it.*

Three things about this claim matter more than the rest.

**PIP will not cover the medical bills.** The Baylor Scott & White balance alone is $13,528.55, above the $10,003 PIP
limit, before the two later emergency visits and any follow-up care are counted. Treat PIP as a partial advance on
living and medical costs, not as the medical recovery, and expect a hospital lien under Texas Property Code chapter 55.

**The uninsured-motorist claim is the main injury recovery, and it does not pay simply because the police report says
the other driver had no proof of insurance.** Under Texas law an insurer owes uninsured-motorist benefits once the
insured establishes the other driver's legal liability and the amount of damages; until that is agreed or determined,
the carrier can withhold payment without breaching the policy. The CR-4 assigns the contributing factors to Unit 1, so
Progressive is holding a report that points at its own insured. Establishing liability, not proving the other driver
was uninsured, is the work here.

**The rideshare question is unresolved in writing.** The loss happened while the client was logged on to the Uber
driver app. Texas Insurance Code sections 1954.151 to 1954.153 permit a personal auto insurer to exclude liability,
PIP, uninsured-motorist, medical-payments, comprehensive and collision coverage while a driver is logged on, with no
duty to defend or indemnify. The declarations page shows Rideshare Use coverage, which is why this claim is open at
all, but the endorsement form is not on file and nothing in writing says how that coverage applies to this loss or
which collision deductible governs. Get that in writing before the total-loss valuation is negotiated.
