Read first — open issues that decide this claim
  1. The CR-4 narrative and diagram attribute fault to Unit 1: “Unit 1 failed to yield right of way while turning left on a flashing yellow light.” The officer’s crash diagram on page 1 draws Unit 1 mid-turn, angled north-east, with its right front corner against Unit 2’s front; his “contributing factors (investigator’s opinion)” block lists two against Unit 1 (failed to yield right of way – turning left; turned when unsafe) and none against Unit 2, and he codes Unit 1’s damage as 3 o’clock “right front quarter”; no charges are recorded; the client says she was facing north and was struck at the centre or rear of the passenger side. Photographs of the damage would settle that. The dashcam stopped recording at impact; the client’s attempt to recover the file is documented in a declaration to be filed in Binder 11, and only post-crash stills survive. The eastbound left-turn signal at this ramp terminal is a four-section flashing-yellow-arrow head (Binder 09 Street View exhibit), so a flashing yellow arrow was possible at that head. Whether it showed a steady green arrow or a flashing yellow arrow at 05:00:11–05:00:15 can only be settled by the signal controller’s time-of-day plan and event logs (requests drafted in Binders 10 and 11). The CR-4 itself codes traffic control as “Signal Light”.
  2. Two claims are open; the Uber claim carries the wrong incident date. Claim 26-854858569 is under the client’s own Progressive policy (declarations page and claim screens in Binder 08). Claim 26-343820011 is the Uber claim: Uber’s in-app notice (Binder 05) states “We work with Progressive to provide protection on your behalf while you operate on the Uber app” and gives that claim number. Its claim summary still shows an incident date of August 27, 2026 (the client says it first showed August 30); the collision was August 28, and the claim rep is to be asked, in writing, to correct it. Still to obtain: the TNC policy forms and any written UM/UIM or PIP rejection (Uber’s certificate states UM/UIM is not included), and Uber’s trip log, since the canceled-trip receipt prints only “5:05 AM” and no accept time. The client’s account (her settled position) is that she had accepted a pickup request at the Aloft Dallas DFW Airport Grapevine and was driving to it (Period 2) and that the rider canceled after the collision; she describes the request as a reservation, and the receipt shows the product only as UberX.
  3. The statement of facts is unsigned (Dossier §6). Counsel should have it executed before it is presented as signed.
  4. Vehicle colour conflict: the CR-4 lists the Atlas as Silver; the repair estimates and the 08/24/2026 photos show it is Black (Deep Black Pearl). A CR-4 correction request may be warranted.
