Personal injury protection is not shown on either page.

## Which period applies
The client's account is that she had accepted a pickup request at the Aloft Dallas DFW Airport Grapevine and was
driving to it when she was struck, and that the rider cancelled after the collision. She describes the request as a
reservation. The receipt in binder 05 shows only "UberX · Aug 28, 2026 · 5:05 AM", an upfront fare of $12.07, $0.00
collected, and pickup and drop-off both printed as "N State Highway 121, Coppell, TX". It prints no request time, no
acceptance time, no cancellation time, no trip ID and no destination.

Under Texas Insurance Code chapter 1954 there are two periods, not three: section 1954.052 covers a driver who is
logged on with no accepted request, and section 1954.053 covers a prearranged ride, which begins at acceptance. Driving
to the pickup and carrying a rider are the same period in Texas. Uber's server-side trip log is the record that settles
which policy responds, and it has been requested by the letters drafted in this binder.

## Assessment
*Analysis prepared for counsel from the documents in this repository. Not legal advice; no attorney has reviewed it.*

**This claim protects other people, not the client's injuries.** Even in the accepted-request period, the $1,000,000
limit is third-party liability cover for people the client might have injured. The only lines opened on her own claim
are non-owned medical payments and non-owned collision, and the certificate rules out uninsured-motorist coverage in
any amount. Since the adverse driver has no proof of financial responsibility on the certified crash report, the injury
recovery has to come from the client's personal uninsured-motorist coverage. This claim is worth pursuing for the
vehicle and for medical payments, not as the injury claim.

**The physical-damage coverage is contingent and expensive.** Collision under policy 06250080 pays actual cash value
less a $2,500 deductible and, per Uber's own insurance page, is contingent on the driver carrying comprehensive and
collision on her personal policy. She does, at a $99 deductible. Where two policies could pay for the same vehicle, the
$99 route is the better one unless the personal carrier declines because she was logged on. Which deductible applies is
still unconfirmed in writing on either claim.

**The wrong incident date is small and worth fixing today.** A claim file that says August 27 while every other record
says August 28 invites a coverage or timeliness argument later, and it is the kind of clerical error that gets harder
to correct once positions harden. Ask for the correction in writing and keep the reply.

**Nothing here is verified by Uber yet.** The accepted request is the client's account. The receipt is consistent with
it but does not prove it. Until Uber's log arrives, every statement to any carrier should say she was logged on, and
give the accepted pickup as her recollection.
